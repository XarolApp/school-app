# Research prompt: loss-framed vs. gain-framed paywalls

Paste this into Cowork.

---

I'm building a paywall for a Czech ed-tech app (ŠkolaMatch — helps 9th graders
pick a high school). Current paywall copy is framed as "pay to unlock" /
"pay to access" (gain framing): the user sees a free result already, and the
paywall offers to unlock more of it.

I heard a UI/UX commentator claim that paywalls convert better when framed as
loss-averse ("you already have this, don't lose it") rather than gain-seeking
("pay to get this"), citing prospect theory / loss aversion (people weigh
losses roughly 2x more than equivalent gains, per Kahneman & Tversky).

I want a light research pass, not exhaustive — just enough to know if this is
worth acting on. Please look into:

1. **Is there real evidence for loss-framed paywall copy outperforming
   gain-framed copy specifically**, as opposed to loss aversion in pricing/
   discounts generally? Distinguish "you already have X, don't lose it" framing
   from adjacent things that also get called loss aversion (scarcity, FOMO,
   countdown timers, "your cart expires") — those are different mechanisms and
   I don't want them conflated.

2. **Any A/B test results, case studies, or academic findings** (mobile app
   paywalls, SaaS trials, subscription products) comparing the two framings
   directly. Flag whether each source is a controlled test or just an
   opinion/blog claim — I want to know how much weight to put on this.

3. **Does this apply to a one-time-use, non-renewing purchase** (season pass,
   not a subscription)? Most loss-aversion paywall examples I've seen are about
   subscription churn/renewal ("don't lose your Pro features") — I'm not sure
   the mechanism transfers to a product used once, ever.

4. **Any known downside or backfire risk** — does loss-framed copy read as
   manipulative or pressuring when the audience may include minors (this
   product's users are 15-year-olds and their parents)? I care about this
   specifically because EU dark-pattern rules (DSA Art. 25, the incoming
   Digital Fairness Act) scrutinize exactly this kind of pressure framing
   aimed at minors — flag if loss-framing crosses into that territory or stays
   clearly on the honest side of it.

Keep it short — a few paragraphs with sources is enough, this isn't a deep
research pass. I mainly want a straight answer: is this worth rewriting the
paywall copy for, and if so, roughly what would the framing change look like
concretely (one or two example rewrites of a line like "Odemkni zbylých X
škol" in the loss-framed direction)?
