# SkolaMatch — 90-Point Founder Context

> **Purpose:** Give this file to another AI before asking it to create SkolaMatch's marketing, pricing, outreach and growth strategy.
>
> **Important:** These are the founder's current answers, beliefs and constraints. They are **not all final decisions**. The next AI should challenge assumptions, research where necessary, and clearly separate founder-provided information from external research, inference, recommendations and experiments.
>
> **Primary objective:** Build the most realistic path from **0 CZK revenue → first paying customer → 100,000 CZK → 500,000 CZK → 1,000,000+ CZK revenue**, while keeping SkolaMatch genuinely useful, trustworthy and scalable.
>
> **Initial market:** Prague, Czech Republic.
>
> **Primary audiences:** Czech 9th-grade students and their parents.

## Point 1 — Business success threshold

Success means making money from the project. A first-season revenue of at least 100,000 CZK would make it a successful project.

## Point 2 — Major first-season target

The ambitious first-season target is over 500,000 CZK revenue.

## Point 3 — Customer milestones

Over 200 paying customers would be good; over 1,000 is the main long-term customer goal.

## Point 4 — Long-term revenue target

The long-term first-year ambition is approximately 1,000,000 CZK revenue, roughly 1,666 customers at an average of 600 CZK.

## Point 5 — Entrepreneurial purpose

This is intended to be the founder's first successful online business and a stepping stone toward larger businesses.

## Point 6 — Revenue priority

Revenue and profit are extremely important. More profitable growth is better than vanity metrics.

## Point 7 — Bootstrapping

The plan is to bootstrap: generate revenue first, then reinvest it into product quality, marketing and eventually hiring.

## Point 8 — Initial cash constraint

Current personal marketing budget is essentially 0 CZK.

## Point 9 — Potential investment

The founder could invest around 10,000 CZK if there is a strong expected return.

## Point 10 — Reinvestment philosophy

The founder is willing to reinvest 100% of early profit if that materially increases growth.

## Point 11 — Initial business model

The initial business model is B2C.

## Point 12 — Initial geography

Launch market is Prague.

## Point 13 — Expansion geography

Planned expansion includes Brno, Plzeň, Ostrava and other major Czech cities.

## Point 14 — Expansion speed

The founder wants to expand quickly once the Prague version is working.

## Point 15 — Product concept

SkolaMatch helps Czech 9th graders choose secondary schools by combining school data, personalized matching, comparison and application guidance.

## Point 16 — Full paid database

Paying users get the full database of Prague schools.

## Point 17 — School browsing

Users can browse schools and open individual school pages.

## Point 18 — School information

School pages organize relevant information that is currently fragmented across school websites and PDFs.

## Point 19 — Open-day information

Users can search for information such as Den otevřených dveří.

## Point 20 — Favorites

Users can save favorite schools.

## Point 21 — School comparison

Users can compare schools next to each other.

## Point 22 — Matching percentage

The product calculates how well each school matches the student's questionnaire answers.

## Point 23 — Mathematical matching

The matching score is intended to be mathematical: matching preferences/features add points to a school's score.

## Point 24 — AI explanation

AI can explain why highly ranked schools fit the student's answers, but AI should not secretly determine the mathematical ranking.

## Point 25 — Personalized identity feeling

The founder wants personalization that makes the product feel like an extension of the student, but it must be grounded in their actual answers rather than fake psychology.

## Point 26 — DiPSy guidance

The product includes or is building a DiPSy guide explaining the application process and what to consider when choosing first, second and third choices.

## Point 27 — Application-order optimization

The founder is considering mathematically optimizing application order using historical acceptance probabilities, but will not launch it if it is unreliable.

## Point 28 — Acceptance prediction

A JPZ-based acceptance-probability feature is being considered, but should only launch if it works well enough to be useful.

## Point 29 — Historical statistics

CERMAT historical data is sourced from official files and is treated as accurate historical data.

## Point 30 — Programme-level statistics

School cards can show averages and school pages can show programme-specific statistics.

## Point 31 — Data sources

Main sources are school websites, Atlas školství and official CERMAT statistics.

## Point 32 — Data maintenance

The founder plans to keep school information as updated as possible.

## Point 33 — Data fragmentation pain

A major problem is that useful information is spread across school websites, different services and PDFs.

## Point 34 — Current user workflow

Students currently may use Atlas školství and then browse individual school websites one by one.

## Point 35 — Decision pain

The product addresses fear of making a bad school decision and wasting time researching schools.

## Point 36 — Student pain

Students face school-choice uncertainty plus pressure to prepare for entrance exams.

## Point 37 — Parent pain

Parents worry that their child will make a bad choice and spend years in an unsuitable school.

## Point 38 — Core outcome

SkolaMatch should save time while helping students find schools that fit them and make a more informed application decision.

## Point 39 — Direct competition

The founder sees few direct competitors offering the exact combined workflow.

## Point 40 — Alternative competitors

Relevant alternatives include Atlas školství, individual school websites, CERMAT statistics, Karierní Kompas and ChatGPT.

## Point 41 — Karierní Kompas view

The founder tested Karierní Kompas and considers its decision-support value weaker than what SkolaMatch aims to provide.

## Point 42 — Competitive positioning

SkolaMatch should be positioned as a specialized school-selection decision system, not merely a school database.

## Point 43 — Trust priority

Trustworthiness is the most important brand attribute.

## Point 44 — Honest rankings

The founder does not want secondary schools paying to receive better ratings or matching scores.

## Point 45 — AI transparency

The product should clearly explain that mathematics creates the ranking and AI explains the result.

## Point 46 — No guaranteed predictions

Historical statistics and future acceptance predictions must never be presented as guarantees.

## Point 47 — Target audiences

Both students and parents are target audiences.

## Point 48 — Primary buyer hypothesis

A parent of a 15-year-old is currently the leading hypothesis for the primary paying buyer.

## Point 49 — Student marketing

Students may be the strongest emotional audience because of exam pressure and school-choice anxiety.

## Point 50 — Parent marketing

Parents may be the strongest payment audience because they often control spending.

## Point 51 — Audience strategy

The founder wants to market to both audiences, with different messages and channels where appropriate.

## Point 52 — Current pricing

Current rough pricing is 250 CZK/month or 700 CZK for what the founder has called lifetime access.

## Point 53 — Meaning of lifetime

What is called lifetime is actually intended as access through the student's current secondary-school application season, so 'season pass' may be more accurate.

## Point 54 — Pricing uncertainty

The founder is unsure whether 700 CZK is too high, too low or optimal and wants research.

## Point 55 — Group pricing idea

A preliminary school-cohort idea is 500 CZK per student for 30 students, producing 15,000 CZK instead of 21,000 CZK.

## Point 56 — Pricing objective

Pricing should be optimized for total revenue/profit, not merely maximum signup conversion.

## Point 57 — Free tier hypothesis

The current plan has no traditional free tier, but the founder is willing to change this if evidence shows a better revenue outcome.

## Point 58 — Pre-payment preview

Before payment, the user currently sees the first matched school, can click its school page, and can see some useful information such as acceptance rate.

## Point 59 — Full paid value

After payment, the user gets the full database and essentially all product features.

## Point 60 — Trial hypothesis

A trial may be offered, with cancellation possible before it ends.

## Point 61 — Onboarding length

The questionnaire currently has about 10 questions and takes roughly 3–5 minutes, potentially under 2 minutes for a fast user.

## Point 62 — Onboarding result

After onboarding, users see a top recommendation and how well it matches them.

## Point 63 — Questionnaire expansion

The founder is willing to add questions if they materially improve recommendation quality.

## Point 64 — Questionnaire redo

Users currently cannot redo the questionnaire, but this can be changed if it improves the product/funnel.

## Point 65 — Testing status

The product has not yet been properly tested with real students.

## Point 66 — Early tester pool

The founder could recruit approximately 20–50 early testers.

## Point 67 — Tester model

A private online test group can receive free access in exchange for meaningful feedback.

## Point 68 — Founder anonymity

The founder wants to remain mysterious publicly and does not want most classmates to know they built the product.

## Point 69 — Public content identity

The founder does not want to show their face or use their own voice publicly.

## Point 70 — Content creation

The founder can use AI and learn editing but currently has no professional editor.

## Point 71 — Social language

Marketing content should be in Czech.

## Point 72 — TikTok readiness

The founder is willing to start a new TikTok account from zero and has not yet run TikTok marketing at scale.

## Point 73 — Instagram/Reels

The founder is also willing to use Instagram/Reels.

## Point 74 — Account strategy

The founder is unsure whether the official account should be highly trustworthy/formal or trend-driven and is open to testing different formats.

## Point 75 — Faceless content

Faceless, no-voice content is preferred and can include strong hooks and trends.

## Point 76 — Trust versus trends

Strong hooks and trends are acceptable, but the brand should not look like generic AI-generated spam.

## Point 77 — Creator outreach

The founder can contact around 100 TikTok creators.

## Point 78 — Affiliate model

Performance-based affiliate marketing is preferred because there is little upfront cash available.

## Point 79 — Affiliate payment

Creators/partners should be paid a commission for actual paying customers.

## Point 80 — Commission flexibility

The founder is willing to offer a substantial commission if it generates more total profit, but does not want an unreasonable share such as 75% without a compelling economic reason.

## Point 81 — Commission duration

The founder wants guidance on one-time versus recurring/season-long commissions.

## Point 82 — Creator concentration

The founder is open to either a small number of creators producing many customers or many creators producing fewer customers; the better economic model should be selected.

## Point 83 — Tutoring partnerships

The founder can contact many tutoring companies because they already reach families preparing for entrance exams.

## Point 84 — Parent outreach

The founder can message approximately 200 parents.

## Point 85 — School partnerships

The founder is willing to contact elementary schools by email or phone.

## Point 86 — School partnership timing

The founder does not want to cold-approach dozens of schools with little evidence of demand; stronger school outreach should follow initial traction.

## Point 87 — School distribution idea

Elementary schools could email 9th-grade parents with an offer for SkolaMatch.

## Point 88 — School trust precedent

The founder observed that parents paid for Karierní Kompas after their school emailed the offer, suggesting institutional trust may improve conversion.

## Point 89 — School payment model

The school does not necessarily need to pay for every student; it could distribute an offer while parents pay individually.

## Point 90 — School cohort model

A school could potentially receive a group discount or group access offer. The founder is open to building a school-facing dashboard if it materially improves distribution. The founder is willing to learn SEO and invest substantial effort if it can produce paying customers. Potential content includes secondary-school guides, school comparisons, admissions information, open days, statistics and application guidance. The founder wants analytics to optimize both revenue and user value. Important events should include landing-page visit, onboarding start/completion, recommendation view, school-page view, paywall view, checkout and payment. Experiments should be judged on revenue/profit and user value, not just conversion rate. A helpful feature that does not increase conversion should generally remain if it has no major downside. Features should be removed or reworked when they create meaningful bugs, confusion, cost or disadvantages without enough value. Stripe payment integration has not yet been implemented. The product is not yet 100% finished. Estimated remaining development time is roughly 7–14 days if no major issues occur. The founder uses Claude Code and can work on marketing while AI-assisted development continues. The founder can work very intensely for short periods to launch but does not want to sacrifice sleep long term. There are no plans to hire immediately; hiring can happen after the business generates meaningful revenue. AI is expected to support many operational tasks during the bootstrapped stage. The founder wants very strong support and plans to listen closely to user bug reports and feature requests. Real user screenshots and genuine reviews are planned as trust-building marketing assets. The founder does not want to sell directly to friends because they may expect free access. Adding a new city may take roughly 1–2 days if data can be collected without manual school outreach. The AI should act as a marketing/growth agent and provide a concrete execution plan rather than generic advice.
